about:
  - "Broadly, generalization involves applying what has been learned in one situation to something new. It is fundamental to learning and adaptive behavior, and is studied across many areas of research, from perception and associative learning to memory, social cognition, language, and artificial intelligence."
  - "Yet these fields often approach generalization using different definitions, concepts, experimental paradigms, and theoretical frameworks. This can make it difficult to see when we are studying closely related phenomena, where our approaches genuinely differ, and what we might learn from one another."
  - "The symposium brings these research traditions into conversation. Each talk introduces how generalization is understood and studied in one area. A structured discussion with the speakers and GADI members then looks at how the perspectives relate: where different fields may use different terms for similar ideas, where the same terms may mean different things, and how different experimental approaches capture different aspects of generalization. GADI will use the discussion as a starting point for a consensus paper on generalization across domains."

speakers:
  - name: "Jessica Lee"
    institution: "University of Sydney"
    photo: "/images/speakers/lee-jessica.jpg"
    bio: >-
      Jessica Lee is a Senior Lecturer in the School of Psychology at the University of Sydney. Her research examines how “high-level” cognitive processes, such as reasoning and rule use, interact with “low-level” associative learning. It aims to uncover general learning principles that translate from animals to humans and from the laboratory to the real world. Her current work focuses on individual differences in the processes underlying fear generalization, with the goal of explaining why some people overgeneralize fear. Her research has been recognized with an ARC Discovery Early Career Researcher Award and an APS Rising Star Award. She is a Fellow of the Psychonomic Society and a former Co-Convenor of the Australian Learning Group.
  - name: "Charley Wu"
    institution: "TU Darmstadt"
    photo: "/images/speakers/wu-charley.jpg"
    photoCredit: "Patrick Bal"
    bio: >-
      Charley Wu is Professor of Computational Cognitive Science at TU Darmstadt, where he directs the Human and Machine Cognition Lab. His research asks how people generalize from limited experience to act in new situations. He develops computational models of learning, exploration, and generalization, and tests them against human behavior and machine learning systems. His ERC Starting Grant project, C⁴, studies how compositional compression shapes cognition and culture. Before Darmstadt, he held positions at the University of Tübingen and Harvard University. He originally trained in philosophy before turning to cognitive science and psychology.
  - name: "Timothy Brady"
    institution: "UC San Diego"
    photo: "/images/speakers/brady-timothy.jpg"
    bio: >-
      Timothy Brady is a Professor in the Department of Psychology at the University of California, San Diego. His research lies at the intersection of visual cognition and memory, using behavioral, computational and neuroimaging methods to understand the limits on our ability to encode and maintain information in visual memory. He has received the Vision Sciences Society Young Investigator Award, the Psychonomic Society Early Career Award, an APS “Rising Star” award, and the APA’s New Investigator Award and Distinguished Scientific Award for Early Career Contributions to Psychology. He received his B.A. in Cognitive Science from Yale, his Ph.D. in Brain and Cognitive Sciences from MIT, and did postdoctoral research at Harvard.

      Susan Gelman is the Heinz Werner Distinguished University Professor of Psychology and Linguistics at the University of Michigan. She is a developmental psychologist whose research focuses on how children's concepts and language develop. Her research has been funded by the National Science Foundation, the National Institutes of Health, the Templeton Foundation, and the Spencer Foundation, among others. She is an elected fellow of the National Academy of Sciences, the American Academy of Arts and Sciences, the Association for Psychological Science, the American Psychological Association (Division 7), and the Cognitive Science Society.

He is a member of the Royal Swedish Academy of Science, member of Academia Europaea, member of Leopoldina Deutsche Akademie für Naturforscher, and member of the Royal Swedish Academy of Engineering Sciences.
